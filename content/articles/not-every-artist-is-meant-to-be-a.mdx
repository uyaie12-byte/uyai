*“What if the music industry’s definition of success is broken?”*

There’s this quiet pressure floating around the music space. If you’re not trending, headlining, or plastered on a billboard, you’re labeled an “underdog” at best, a “failure” at worst. But what if some artists aren’t built for billboards? What if their power lies in staying small, strange, and intentional?

### Cult Followings over Mass Appeal

Not every artist is for everyone. They’re built for the ones who really get it. Think Cruel Santino. Does he make “mainstream” music? Not at all. But has he shaped an entire wave? Absolutely. His world isn’t for the casual listener. It’s for fans who want to live in a different universe.

The same goes for Tems, who, even with global fame, never rushed to play the industry game. Her early works like “Try Me” and “The Key” weren't crafted for virality. They were textured and spiritual, far from the usual mainstream music. She didn’t chase the spotlight. It found her, years later.

Beyond Nigeria, artists like Little Simz or Noname have built an audience who listen. Not scroll, not skip. No gimmicks or dance challenges. Just music with depth and an audience that respects them for it.

### The Industry's Blind spot

The industry speaks in numbers: streams, followers, playlist placements. Anything that can be charted or tracked. If you don't hit 100k streams, you're deemed “not serious” or worse.

But the algorithm isn't a music fan. It doesn't understand intention, only performance. So what happens to artists who value storytelling over trendiness? Mood over moments?

They get ignored. Or worse, they get told to “rebrand.”

### We Don’t Let Artists Breathe Anymore

Part of this problem is the constant spotlight. Artists are expected to be consistent, visible and ready for anything. There’s no room to evolve publicly. No “figuring it out” phases. You’re either blowing or invisible.

But growth isn't loud. Some artists need years in the shadows to experiment, fail, and refine their craft. But the industry’s 24/7 content grind doesn't really care. Their work might not trend today but could redefine the culture in five years’ time.

### Let the Quiet Ones Build

The real failure isn't in an artist avoiding the spotlight. It’s in forcing artists who are introverts to be extroverted. Forcing them to do interviews when they’d rather be in the studio. Telling them to post more, tweet more, do more, when their art should be enough.

We need to leave room in the culture for different kinds of success. Artists who care less about the spotlight and more about what their music feels like in headphones, late at night.

Everyone wants a core fanbase, But not everyone has to be a trending topic..

### Finally

Let some artists just exist. Let them make art without feeling like they’ve failed for not being mainstream. There’s power in that freedom. Because not everyone is meant to shine upfront. Some people are here to shift the culture from the background and sometimes, that’s even louder.
