International Women’s Day came and went. As usual, timelines were flooded with pink branded “Happy Women’s Day” posts, a few more “Women in Music” playlists dropped and hashtags trended. Very Cute.

But here’s the deal: the conversation around women in music (artist, producer, execs) shouldn’t be limited to a single day of hypes and performances.

*As tems said: “They breadcrumb you because they live on breadcrumbs… You’ve become addicted to breadcrumbs.”*

Women constantly face an industry that judges them on everything but their music; their outfits, their looks, how well they "play the game”. Yet they’re still out here fighting for the visibility, respect, and opportunities they’ve always deserved.

So, let’s talk.

---

### The Numbers Don’t Lie, And They’re Not Pretty

Every year, we hear about how the industry is “making progress” or being more “inclusive” but the stats still tell a different story.

A USC Annenberg study found women make up just 30% of artists, 14% of songwriters, and a depressing 4% of producers on top-charting songs.

And in Naija? It's literally worse.

The industry is still heavily male-dominated, and while women are breaking barriers, they’re doing it in an environment that constantly tests them in ways men will never experience.

In an interview with Korty EO, Tems spoke on the realities of being a female artist… and revealed that she wears baggy clothes to the studio so people focus on her music, not her body. Imagine having to downplay your style, just to be seen as a “professional” in a room full of men.

And Tems isn’t alone. Women in music are forced to think about things that men never have to; their appearance, how they move, and whether their success will be credited to their talent or to a male co-sign.

---

### The Double Standards Are LOUD

-A male artist wears something chaotic? “He’s iconic.”

A female artist wears something chaotic? “She’s trying too hard”. In fact let’s flip it, and if she shows skin, suddenly the conversations become about her body and not her music.

-A male artist drops a club banger about money and sex? It’s a banger.

A female artist does the same? Suddenly, it’s “she’s not serious” or “she should sing about deeper things like love”.

-A male artist takes a break? “He’s rebranding.”

A female artist takes a break? “She fell off.”

-A male artist is assertive? “Boss.”

A female artist is assertive? "difficult to work with.”

Women work twice as hard for half the credit. And when they win? They’re asked, “What’s it like being a female artist?” as if their gender is a genre🤦‍♀️.

---

### The Erasure of Women’s Contributions in Music

Women have pushed music forward for decades, yet their impact is often not mentioned or downplayed. In Afrobeats, the legends are almost always men (am I lying?). But where’s the love for artists like Onyeka Onwenu, Asa, Lijadu Sisters?

Even globally, you’ll hear names like Michael Jackson and Prince being referenced as music pioneers, but where’s the same energy for Missy Elliott or Janet Jackson, women who redefined their genres?

History forgets women unless we scream their names.

---

### Women Are Running Things( even if the Industry Won’t admit it)

Even with the gender barrier, women in music are thriving.

‼️Tems isn't just Wizkid’s feature, she’s a Grammy-winning songwriter for Beyonce and Rihanna and is the only Nigerian artist with an Oscars. Purr💋

‼️Ayra Starr is the first African female artist to win the Best International Act at the 2025 MOBO Awards, and also the first woman in 16 years to win the Best African Music Act.

‼️ Bloody Civilian co-produced “Wake up” which was for the Marvel film "Black Panther: Wakanda Forever".

‼️ Behind the scenes, Rima Tahini, Jada P, and Stephanie Okocha are shaping the sound of Nigeria’s biggest stars.

Women aren’t just part of the conversation, I can say for a fact that they are taking charge and leading it.

---

### What Needs to Change?

If we really want to support women in music, it has to go beyond all these International Women’s Day posts.

Give them the same opportunities as men, no more excuses, all that emotional crap.

Stop the lazy comparisons, women are allowed to exist as individuals, not just as “the female version” of some male.

Credit their work properly, producers, songwriters, engineers. Women are in every part of the industry, and their contributions deserve to be recognized.

Support beyond streaming, buy tickets, show up at their shows, and scream their music like you do for the men.

---

### This Conversation Doesn’t Expire

Women in music shouldn't come up like it's a trend or something. Their impact is daily, their talent is undeniable, and their presence in the industry is a necessity.

So yeah, I missed International Women’s Day but I won’t miss the chance to talk about women in music every other day of the year. And neither should you.
