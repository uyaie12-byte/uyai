OSÁ's *FEELS* is a short project about a very familiar kind of mess: caring about someone, losing your way in it, and eventually finding yourself again.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!dNBs!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F45307e9c-9b36-4a9d-9a9d-097a6a92f78e_2289x3433.jpeg)

<figcaption>OSÁ</figcaption>

The seven-track project moves through love, confusion, vulnerability and growth without trying to turn any of them into a grand story. At just over 15 minutes, it feels more like a snapshot of a period in OSÁ's life than a project determined to explain itself.

That works for it.

The songs move between African rhythms, R&B and hip-hop, giving OSÁ enough room to play with different textures without the project feeling scattered. **LINGO**, featuring Toyé, is the obvious emotional turning point, arriving in the middle of the tracklist as things begin to feel a little less heavy. By the time you get to **PARTÉ**, featuring Gidiboybenji and Meday, the project has travelled somewhere different from where it started.

There's a strong sense of collaboration throughout, too. Stomy appears on **GARAGWÉ**, Aema and Nosa join OSÁ on **TIME**, and Larz features on **E YÉ**. But the guests never make *FEELS* feel overcrowded. Seven songs is enough space to introduce each moment and get out before it overstays its welcome.

This is OSÁ's second full project, following 2023's *Roll Bounce*, and it comes after five years of building independently. That history is worth keeping in mind because *FEELS* doesn't sound like an artist trying to introduce himself. It sounds like someone further along, more comfortable letting the music sit in its own ambiguity.

Even the release experience follows that instinct. Ahead of the project, OSÁ held a phone-free listening session in Brooklyn where writing prompts and communal reflection replaced the usual industry-party formula. It makes sense for a project this inward-looking.

*FEELS* doesn't promise to solve anything. It just sits with the feeling long enough for you to recognise it.

And sometimes, that's enough.
