There’s this thing that has been happening lately where a new song comes on and you immediately go "wait, I know this"?

That's been happening a lot in 2025.

I didn’t think much of it at first, but by the third or fourth time it happened, I realized… wait, is everyone suddenly digging into the 2000s again?

It clicked when Adekunle Gold dropped ‘Coco Money’ and Rihanna’s ‘Bitch Better Have My Money’ came through midway with a mad twist. Not a full sample, but the attitude was unmistakable. Then Styl-Plus resurfaced with a new take on ‘Olufunmi’ with Ladipoe, which took me back a couple of years but still felt so fresh. Even globally, Summer Walker built her recent album *Finally Over It* on the same foundation.

Afrobeats is in this same wave. Take Asake's ‘Active’ with Travis Scott. The track interpolates Jazzman Olofin's 2004 hit ‘Raise Da Roof’, a song that defined Nigerian club culture back then. Now it’s connecting with people who weren’t even born yet.

Ayra Starr is another example. ‘Gimme Dat’ samples Mary J. Blige and Wyclef Jean's ‘911’. Even Rema brought Sade’s ‘Is It a Crime’ into his single “Baby (Is it a crime)”. These aren’t random throwbacks, Artists are reaching for the sounds that shaped them, sounds they grew up with when Nigerian music was still shaping its identity.

And it’s not just Afrobeats. Globally, early-2000s R&B is having a full-blown revival. Summer Walker’s Finally Over It might as well be titled I Miss the 90s. She sampled Beyoncé’s ‘Yes’ on ‘No’, Mariah Carey’s ‘Always Be My Baby’ on ‘Baby’, Bobby Womack on ‘Get Yo Boy’ Roberta Flack and Donny Hathaway on ‘Baller’. She basically built a time machine out of R&B samples.

Why now? Part of it’s the 20-year nostalgia cycle. Part of it’s the fatigue from the endless chase for “newness.” Part of it’s that clearing samples is easier than it used to be. But more than all that, it’s the power of familiarity. The 2000s weren’t obsessed with perfection; the music was raw and honest. Hearing them reimagined connects millennials…making them feel seen, while introducing the newer generation to a whole catalog they didn't know they needed.

This is how culture moves. Not by abandoning the past, but by reinterpreting it.

And I don’t think this wave is just nostalgia. It feels like artists reconnecting with the music that raised them. Rema grew up hearing Sade. Asake danced to Jazzman Olofin. Summer Walker probably had Always Be My Baby on repeat in middle school. They’re not exploiting nostalgia, they’re sharing it. Saying, “This shaped me. I want you to hear it too.”

Maybe that’s why these records are landing so hard. People don’t just want new things, they want something that feels like something. And right now, that feeling is coming from the classics.

The 2000s gave us music that lasted. Songs people still know word-for-word. Melodies that still make you stop and feel something. And in 2025, artists are tapping back into that, not because they can't create something new, but because they understand that some foundations are worth building on.

This isn’t just for nostalgia’s sake, It’s respect. It’s a generation saying ‘we see where we came from, and we’re taking it forward.’
